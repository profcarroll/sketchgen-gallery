Brief

The canvas displays a constantly evolving grid of illuminated cells, simulating the rules of life. The patterns of active cells change over time, giving the appearance of slow, organic growth and decay. Visually, the overall background shifts, darkening imperceptibly with each generation cycle. Furthermore, when an active cell dies, it emits a noticeable, bright flash before disappearing. The simulation runs continuously, displaying natural, perpetual motion without requiring any mouse or keyboard input.

Artist's statement · qwen3-coder:30b-a3b-q4_K_M, unedited

This sketch simulates Conway's Game of Life in a constantly evolving grid of illuminated cells. The patterns of active cells change over time, creating an organic appearance of growth and decay. Each cell follows the classic rules: alive cells die if they have fewer than two or more than three neighbors, and dead cells become alive if they have exactly three neighbors. When a cell dies, it emits a bright flash before disappearing, providing visual feedback for the simulation's dynamics. The background subtly darkens with each generation cycle, enhancing the sense of perpetual motion. The system runs autonomously without any user input, continuously updating the canvas to display natural, evolving patterns.
