This sketch presents a forest of ancient, self-sustaining tree forms that embody the cyclical processes of structural stress and crystalline growth described in the brief. The trees are composed of trunk segments, root systems, and branching structures that respond dynamically to simulated forces, creating an organic architecture where geometric elements warp under visible stress. The work captures the moment when structural integrity begins to fracture—branches break off, roots twist and extend, and the entire form dissolves into particulate mist. This process is not destruction but transformation: particles emerge from stress points, forming new crystalline structures that grow in harmony with the decayed framework. The muted teal and luminescent color palette shifts subtly across these transformations, mapping the dynamic forces of both destruction and reformation. Each tree exists in a constant state of flux, exhibiting gravity-defying hyperbolic curves as it ages and undergoes stress-induced morphological changes.
