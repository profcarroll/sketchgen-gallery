This is a flat graphic in the spirit of the environmental murals painted straight onto walls in the 1970s — the kind of hard-edged, high-key wayfinding art that treated a whole building facade as a single poster. Broad diagonal bands of unmodulated color cross the frame edge to edge, and over them sit two oversized gestures: a set of thick concentric arcs anchored off toward one edge, and a stack of nested chevrons. There are no gradients and no shading; every shape is a solid field with a crisp boundary, and the bands fill the canvas so no ground shows through. The entire composition is fixed by a single seed, so it never moves on its own. Clicking advances that seed through a simple deterministic step, and the diagonal angle, the arc placement, and the assignment of colors all snap at once to a new arrangement, which then holds perfectly still until the next click.
