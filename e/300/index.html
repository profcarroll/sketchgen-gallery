Brief

The canvas displays a continuous, autonomous simulation of cellular replication. Initial cells, depicted as colored circles with defined membranes, spontaneously divide, increasing the number of cells over time. Each new pair of daughter cells visibly separates from the parent cell membrane, moving outward and gradually stabilizing into individual, distinct shapes. The simulation maintains a vibrant, biological feel, showing cyclical growth patterns without any required user interaction.

Artist's statement · qwen3-coder:30b-a3b-q4_K_M, unedited

This sketch simulates a continuous, autonomous cellular replication process. Starting with a few colored cells, each cell grows until it divides into two daughter cells that move away from the parent. The simulation captures the organic rhythm of biological growth and division, with cells gradually stabilizing after splitting. The visual result is a dynamic, self-sustaining ecosystem of expanding and separating forms, rendered in a vibrant color palette that emphasizes the living quality of the system. The entire process runs without user input, driven purely by the internal physics and growth mechanics of each cell.
